### 1.2 H1's fix, verified exhaustively rather than by fixture

Previous cycles validated this with seven hand-chosen fixtures. I replicated the two selection loops verbatim from `20260720170000` into a `pg_temp` function and brute-forced **every** multiset of 1–4 buckets with counts 1–12:

**Zero violations across all three invariants.** The construction holds over the shape space, not merely over the fixtures chosen to test it. This is the first assertion in the phase that is exhaustive rather than exemplary, and it is what moves H1 from "fixed on the reported cases" to "fixed".

The repository's own regression test (`p46a-analytics-rpc-isolation.test.ts:413`, *"is not reversible by combining the two tools a caller always holds"*) is correctly scoped over the **pair** of tools rather than the one function — the first assertion in the phase written over the caller's mount, which is the scope the invariant has. ## 2. Cross-tool and multi-turn inference (the areas specifically requested)

### 2.1 Cross-tool privacy guarantees — holds

The invariant review #3 asked for — *no two tools available to the same caller may jointly determine a suppressed cell* — is now true, and true for a structural reason rather than by arithmetic coincidence: the residual the subtraction recovers **is** the aggregate the payload publishes outright. There is nothing left for a sibling tool to reveal, because nothing is being withheld that a total could confirm.

I checked the other direction too — whether any *other* mounted tool re-publishes a patient-attribute distribution by a different route. It does not. `ai_get_appointment_stats` groups appointments by status/doctor/department (no patient attributes, correctly no suppression — appointment volume per doctor is not PHI and these roles see the calendar). `ai_get_clinic_summary` publishes counts, a department directory, and an active-doctor directory — all non-clinical and already visible to admin/manager in the UI. The operational list tools return patient rows these roles administer directly. No second path to a blood-type distribution exists.

### 2.2 Multi-turn inference resistance — closed by construction, and this retires review #3's I4 warning

Review #3's I4 predicted the next scope up would be the multi-turn conversation, *"where a range can be varied and results accumulated across turns,"* and warned that nothing bounded it. **The H1 fix closed that vector as a side effect, and this is worth stating explicitly because the warning is still carried forward in the plan and in the cycle-3 fixes report §8.**

The distribution in `buckets_all_time` is computed with **no range predicate at all** (the `grouped` CTE filters only on `clinic_id` and `is_deleted`), and `patients_total` is likewise all-time. Only `patients_new_in_range` respects `p_start`/`p_end`. `p_group_by` admits exactly three values. Therefore the distribution payload is **deterministic given `group_by`**: varying the range across turns returns byte-identical buckets, and accumulating results across turns yields nothing a single turn does not. The field is honestly named `bucket_scope: 'all_time'` and the tool description tells the model not to describe the buckets as belonging to the range.

The three groupings are three marginals of the same population and do not compose into a joint distribution. Within a session there is no time-varying input to exploit. The residual — population drift across *days* — is not a session-scoped attack and is unreachable in any case for a role that can read `patients` directly.

**Recommendation (documentation only, not a fix):** the plan §1122 and `P4_6_PHASE_REVIEW_CYCLE3_FIXES.md` §8 should be updated when convenient to record that the multi-turn scope was closed by the H1 fix, so P4.7 does not inherit a warning that no longer applies. P4.7 must still not *assume* it — a future tool that makes a distribution range-dependent would reopen it.

### 2.3 Residual, and the honest limit of generalization — Info I1

One disclosure survives, and it is inherent to k-anonymity generalization rather than to this implementation.

When the category domain is **enumerable and closed** — `blood_type` is; `department` and `assigned_doctor` are, via the directories `ai_get_clinic_summary` publishes — the categories folded into `Other` can be identified **by elimination** from the visible bucket list. Combined with `grouped_bucket_count`, a caller learns *"these specific N categories together total M"*, and since a category with zero patients never appears in a `group by` at all, each folded category is bounded to `[1, M−(N−1)]`.

This is weaker than what it replaced (review #3's reproduction recovered `A+ = 4, B+ = 3` **exactly**), and the floor guarantees `M ≥ 5` and `N ≥ 2`, so no individual is identified. It is the standard, accepted cost of generalization. It is recorded here because §1055's phrasing — aggregates "cannot be reversed into individuals" — is true, while a looser reading ("nothing at all is learned about a hidden category") is not, and the difference should be on record rather than rediscovered by a fifth review. **No action required.** Admin and manager can read `patients` rows through RLS and compute the true distribution themselves; suppression here is a control on what leaves the platform for a third-party inference provider, and at that boundary an exact pair-sum is a materially different disclosure from two exact cells.

## 3. Low Findings

### L1 (new) — The literal `'Other'` aggregate bucket can collide with a tenant-authored category name

`supabase/migrations/20260720170000_…sql` (the aggregate emit block, `'bucket', 'Other'`), and the `grouped` CTE's `coalesce(d.name, 'Unassigned')` / `coalesce(doc.full_name, 'Unassigned')`

The aggregate bucket is emitted with the hardcoded label `Other`. Department names and staff full names are tenant-authored, so a clinic may legitimately have a department named "Other" (or "Unassigned", which has the same issue and predates P4.6). When it does, `buckets_all_time` contains **two buckets with the same `bucket` value** — one real and visible, one the generalized aggregate — in the same array, in the same grouping.

Structurally they are still distinguishable: the aggregate carries `suppressed: true`, `suppression_reason: 'aggregated'`, and a non-null `grouped_bucket_count`, and the tool description tells the model to key off `suppression_reason`. So this is not a privacy defect — the aggregate does not become decomposable, and nothing hidden becomes visible. It is a **presentation and honesty risk**: a model summarizing a list with two identically-labelled rows is likely to merge them or to attribute the aggregate's count to the real department, which is precisely the class of "loose summary silently drops a caveat" that P4.6B's structured-notice rendering exists to prevent.

Cheapest correct fixes, in preference order: (a) reserve the label at the query layer — emit the aggregate under a sentinel that cannot be a tenant string (e.g. `__aggregated__`) and let the UI and prompt render it; or (b) keep `Other` but have the emit loop detect a collision with a visible bucket and disambiguate. Option (a) also removes the untranslated English literal from a payload that is otherwise locale-neutral. **Recommended, may be scheduled.**

## 4. Architecture, Security, Authorization, Entitlements & RLS

**The five-authority model holds and each layer denies independently.** Verified at the database rather than read: every P4.6 `SECURITY DEFINER` function is `stable`; the two normalizers and `ai_permission_is_grantable` are `immutable`; the four ranked search RPCs are correctly `SECURITY INVOKER` so patient RLS applies; and the ACLs match the migrations exactly, with no surviving `PUBLIC` grant and `effective_ai_feature` reachable only from inside a DEFINER guard (`{postgres=X/postgres}`).

**Identity is server-derived throughout.** No tool accepts a clinic id, actor id, or role; no aggregate RPC takes a tenant parameter — each re-resolves `auth_clinic_id()` and `auth_role()` itself through `ai_assert_analytics_caller`, whose three scopes (`operational` / `clinic_analytics` / `financial`) mirror the three application role sets in `lib/ai/authorization.ts`, which in turn mirror the registry's `roles` arrays. Three independent statements of the same matrix, each of which denies alone. `verifiedId` in `entity-filters.ts` closes the model-asserted-uuid path for both doctor and department filters.

**Deny-by-default is structural.** A registry entry cannot exist without declaring `roles`, `requiredFeatures`, and `taskClasses`; `resolveToolMount` reads all three; every `execute()` re-asserts. I checked all 15 entries against their tools' internal gates for the review-#1 H1 class (a registry gate looser than its execute gate) and found none.

**Entitlements and the per-user grant.** `assertFinancialInsightsAccess` re-runs role → staff spine → `ai.staff_analytics` → `ai.financial_insights` → per-user grant on **every** invocation, not only at mount. `hasAiUserPermission` fails closed on lookup error. The grant table's composite FK to `profiles(id, clinic_id)` is the real cross-tenant control (correctly identified as such in the migration's own comment, which also correctly names the incidental one), the clinic-leading PK is the modelling correction, and `is_primary_clinic_admin` + `ai_permission_is_grantable` enforce the primary-admin and grantable-role rules in the policy's `using` **and** `with check`. `actions/ai-permissions.ts` states the same two rules at the application layer, and additionally refuses a grant against a plan lacking the entitlement.

**Tool registry, capability resolution, tool mounting.** `lib/ai/capabilities.ts` deriving affordances from `resolveToolMount` — the model's own resolution — remains the strongest structural decision in the phase; the panel and the tool array cannot drift because there is one resolution. The deliberate omission of `taskClass` (returning the union as a UI superset) is correct and documented. The `unavailable` state and `explainFinancialAbsence`'s re-read of entitlement-then-grant keep `not_entitled` and `not_granted` as separately actionable states, which is what sends a manager to the right person.

**Prompt-injection protections.** The three-layer model is unchanged and still honestly framed — `untrusted-text.ts` states plainly that neutralization reduces blast radius and is not what makes the system safe. `harden()` at the mount boundary covers every current and future tool by construction; the field-aware cap is matched on the key (which we author) and never the value (which tenants author); `text_truncated_fields` closes the silent-truncation gap for length; `isSystemAuthored` correctly gives denials and candidate-free clarifications `system_generated:` provenance rather than the tenant framing; and `withProvenance` reserves rather than overwrites. `DENIAL_GUIDANCE` enumerating every `AiToolDenialReason` so a new one is a compile error is the right construction and mirrors `ERROR_COPY_KEYS` on the client half.

**Entity resolution (P4.6C).** The database is authoritative for matching and ranking; `lib/ai/entity-search.ts` only prepares query variants and classifies scores. `classifyConfidence` requires both a strong top score **and** a clear lead, so namesakes always force a clarification — the model never fuzzy-matches names itself. PHI egress is correctly minimized: full `phone`/`email` only for a single `high`-confidence match, `contact_details_withheld` on the payload, and the tool description explains the absence so the model does not appear to have lost data.

### Info

**I2 — `list_pending_followups` is the one tool whose per-tool assert is looser than its registry entry.** The registry excludes managers (`roles: ["admin", "receptionist"]`) because `get_followups_dashboard` denies them, but the tool's own `execute()` calls `assertAnalyticsToolAccess`, which admits managers. I verified the database independently: `get_followups_dashboard` contains `if v_role = 'manager'::public.user_role then raise …`. So two independent authorities still deny — registry non-mount and the RPC — and the pattern is documented in the tool's header comment. Recorded because it is the sole entry where the *middle* layer does not restate the rule, and because a future refactor that replaced the RPC would silently remove one of the two.

**I3 — A transient permission-lookup failure presents as `not_granted`.** `hasAiUserPermission` returns `false` on a database error (correctly — a read that did not succeed is not a grant), and `explainFinancialAbsence` therefore reports `not_granted`, whose copy tells the user to ask their administrator to enable it. The denial is right; only the attributed *cause* may be wrong during an outage. The tool path is unaffected — it has a distinct `lookup_failed` reason with its own copy. Cosmetic, low frequency, no action.

## 8. Summary

Three fix cycles have taken this phase from architecturally strong to genuinely finished. The database boundary enforces role, entitlement, per-user grant, and the primary-admin rule, each verified live rather than inferred. The error transport is understood and handled correctly across all three channels, pinned by tests that drive the real SDK. The audit ledger covers every invocation class at the one boundary that cannot be forgotten. Entity resolution never lets the model guess a name. Identity is server-derived everywhere, and the role matrix is stated three times by three authorities that each deny alone.

The phase's oldest finding — small-cell suppression, raised in review #1 and re-raised at a new scope in each of #2 and #3 — is closed. What makes this cycle different from the previous two is the shape of the fix rather than its correctness: instead of protecting a secret at one more layer, the cycle-3 fix removed the need for the secret. Suppressed categories are generalized into one unnamed aggregate whose count is safe to publish exactly, so the total goes back to being exact, the two tools agree instead of contradicting each other, and the subtraction an attacker performs now returns a figure the payload already states. Two Mediums, a Low, and — as this review establishes — the multi-turn vector the previous review predicted would appear next all fell out of that one change. I brute-forced 28,560 distribution shapes against the selection loops the construction rests on and found zero violations of the three invariants it claims.

The remaining items are one new Low about a bucket label that can collide with a tenant-authored name, four deferred Lows with sound stated reasons, and a deployment window that needs scheduling. None of them is a required fix, and none of them is a reason to hold the phase.

**P4.6 is approved for production.**
